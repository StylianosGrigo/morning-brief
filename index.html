🎲 Section 5 — Today's Wildcard

Karamanlis's 11-Year Paris Exile — The Original Athens-Paris Operating Model (1963–1974)

When Constantine Karamanlis was forced out of power in 1963, he chose Paris. Not London, not Washington — Paris. For 11 years, he lived in a quiet apartment near Champ-de-Mars, maintained relationships across French political and business circles, studied European integration from the inside, and positioned Greece for the EEC when the moment came. When the military junta fell in July 1974, he was summoned back to Athens and, within 7 years, delivered Greece's EEC accession (1981). The two-city operating model — Athens as home base, Paris as the European relationship layer — has a 50-year track record in Greek statecraft.

What's less known: Karamanlis credited his Paris years not as exile but as his most productive intellectual period. He wasn't waiting — he was building the context engineering that let him move fast when conditions changed.

You're running STIQ from Athens while building a Paris presence — the same dual-city playbook Karamanlis ran, but in venture-backed agentic software rather than politics. The precedent suggests that Paris patience pays off on a longer timescale than most founders expect. It's not a market you enter; it's a relationship you compound. Also: the EU-US Turnberry Agreement ratifying in 9 days is, in a small way, the latest chapter of what Karamanlis started in 1981.
