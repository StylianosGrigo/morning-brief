🎲 Today's Wildcard
The Diolkos: Ancient Corinth's 6km Toll Road That Ran Profitably for 1,300 Years

Around 600 BC, Corinth built a paved stone trackway called the Diolkos — a 6km track of grooved limestone slabs — to drag ships across the isthmus between the Aegean and Ionian seas. Ships paid a toll to skip a 700km coastal journey around the Peloponnese. The Diolkos required no ships of its own: just a track, rollers, and winches.

It operated continuously from ~600 BC to ~700 AD — 1,300 years of profitable infrastructure. Corinth became the wealthiest city in Greece not because it made things, but because it owned the crossing.
