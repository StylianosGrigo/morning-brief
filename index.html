The Antikythera Mechanism — the world's first "analog agent"

A bronze, ~30-gear hand-cranked computer recovered from a shipwreck off Antikythera, built in Greece around 150–100 BC. Turn one dial and it autonomously computes the positions of the sun, moon and planets, predicts eclipses, and tracks the four-year Olympiad cycle — a thousand years ahead of anything comparable. It's effectively a deterministic agent: encode the rules of the cosmos once, then let the machine run the inference.

Why this for you today: you build agentic systems for a living and you're Greek — this is the oldest known instance of "embed the model, crank the handle, get the answer." A nice reframe for how you talk about agents: the Greeks shipped the first one 2,100 years ago. Read
