🎲 Today's Wildcard
The Philiki Etairia VC Model: How Greek Diaspora Merchants Funded the 1821 Revolution — The World's First Diaspora Investment Network

In 1814, three Greeks in Odessa (a merchant, a teacher, a clerk) founded the Philiki Etairia — the "Society of Friends" — a secret network that, over 7 years, enrolled 1,000+ members across Odessa, Bucharest, Constantinople, London, and Paris. The network's primary function: raise capital and coordinate action to fund the Greek War of Independence.

The structure was explicitly like a VC network: tiered membership (scouts, operators, initiates, archons), geographic distribution, pseudonyms, blind trust, and capital commitments proportional to wealth and conviction. Merchant members in Odessa, Vienna, and Paris committed fortunes to a venture most people said was impossible. The payoff horizon was 10+ years. There was no liquidity event other than freedom.

By 1821, the Etairia had built a distributed intelligence and capital network spanning the entire diaspora — before the telegraph, before modern finance, before anything resembling a communications infrastructure.

Why this for you today: You split time between Athens and Paris. You're building STIQ in a country where the tech ecosystem was partially seeded by diaspora Greeks returning home — same structure as 1821. The "Greek Agentic Operators Network" idea that got skipped a few weeks ago failed because it was framed as a marketplace. The Philiki Etairia framing is better: not a marketplace, but a trust network of operators with shared identity and skin in the game. The topology of 1814 Odessa looks a lot like the topology of 2026 Athens-Paris-Thessaloniki. Worth a read: Filiki Eteria — Wikipedia ↗
