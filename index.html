The Agora Was a Food Hall: Athens Invented the Modern Restaurant Economy 2,500 Years Before Yelp

Tsipras chose Thisseio tonight — steps from the Hephaestus Temple and literally on top of the Ancient Agora — and the choice reveals something most people miss: the Agora wasn't primarily a political space. It was a food market first.

Fish sellers, bread bakers, olive oil merchants, wine traders — all operating in an open-air clustered market before the philosophers arrived. The political debates happened around the food stalls, not instead of them. Socrates famously lingered near the fish market. The exchange of ideas and the exchange of food were the same thing.

The structural pattern of the Agora — hyper-local, relationship-driven, owner-operated, premium-within-the-cluster — is the same structural pattern of Greek restaurants today: 90K+ restaurants, 87% owner-operated, zero franchise dominance. That's not a backwardness. It's path-dependency from a 2,500-year-old market design.
