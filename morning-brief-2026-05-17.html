📚 Historical / cultural data point
The Minoan Trading Network: the world's first supply chain protocol — and it maps exactly to what STIQ is building

3,700 years ago, the Minoans (Bronze Age Crete, 1700–1450 BCE) operated the Mediterranean's first documented long-distance supply chain. They had trading posts across Cyprus, Egypt, mainland Greece, and the Levant. They used standardized weights and measures. They tracked commodity flows — olive oil, wine, textiles, copper — across political borders with clay tablets (Linear A). The whole thing ran without centralized control: a distributed coordination system with local nodes and shared protocols.

Sound familiar? It's exactly the architecture STIQ is building for Greek F&B ops: standardized data protocols across distributed restaurant nodes, real-time inventory and ordering flows, BoH coordination across brands. The Minoans did it with clay tablets and ships. You're doing it with Claude Code and APIs. Same problem, 3,700 years apart.

The Minoan network collapsed when Santorini erupted (c. 1628 BCE) and their trading hubs went dark. The lesson isn't the collapse — it's that the protocol survived and was absorbed into Mycenaean and later Greek trading culture. Resilient protocols outlast any single operator.

💡 Why this for you today: You just went through an ND Congress week where "Greece 2030" was framed as a break from the past. But Greece's real competitive advantage isn't legislation — it's 3,700 years of Mediterranean supply chain culture encoded into its operators. STIQ is a protocol, not just a product. Build accordingly.
