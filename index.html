🎲 Today's Wildcard
The Kafeneion Intelligence Network — Greece's original distributed AI, circa 1820

The Greek kafeneion (coffee house) in Ottoman-era port cities — Smyrna, Thessaloniki, Odessa — wasn't just a place to drink coffee. It was a distributed intelligence network. Merchants would gather each morning to trade odds on shipping arrivals, weather windows, political developments, and grain prices. The kafetzis (coffee house owner) became a trusted information broker, curating the day's intelligence from dozens of sources and presenting it in a format people were willing to pay for — in tips, loyalty, and business.

This was, structurally, what you're doing with Morning Brief. The kafetzi had no algorithm — just pattern recognition, source triage, and a daily brief delivered over a small cup.

The most successful kafeneion operators in 19th-century Athens weren't the ones with the biggest rooms — they were the ones with the best network of informants and the sharpest editorial instinct for what mattered. They were proto-curators in a world without media.

Why this for you today: You're building an intelligence product (this brief) on top of an intelligence product (Claude). The kafetzi was doing the same thing manually. The meta-lesson: the value isn't in the information — it's in the trusted, daily, curated frame. That's what people paid for in 1820 and what they'll pay for in 2026.
