The French Boulangerie Paradox: 35,000 artisan bakeries, 98% survival rate, €800K-1M revenue each

France has 35,000 artisan boulangeries, most under 5 employees. Regulated by the Décret Pain of 1993: bread must be made on premises to be called "artisan." They operate on geographic spacing requirements (one per neighborhood zone), fixed-hours rota, and a guild apprenticeship system. The result:

  • 98% year-1 survival rate vs. ~40% for US restaurants
  • €800K-1M average annual revenue with 3-5 staff
  • Average transaction: €3-4 — but customers come 5-7x per week
  • No loyalty programs. No app. No delivery. Just frequency by proximity + trust.

This is the opposite of CAVA's model — and it also works at national scale. Two sustainable food business models, completely different mechanisms.

Wikipedia: Boulangerie →

🎯 Why this for you today: CAVA just reported +32.2% revenue and proved the Mediterranean bowl scales to $434M/quarter through traffic and SSS growth. But across the Mediterranean, the artisan frequency model (French bakery, Greek neighborhood taverna) also works — not at CAVA's scale, but at near-100% survival and a very different risk profile. As someone building at the intersection of tech and Mediterranean food, this tension is worth sitting with: scale vs. frequency. Also: you visit Paris regularly. The boulangerie on your street is running a €800K business with 2 employees, no VC, and no app. That's worth understanding structurally.
